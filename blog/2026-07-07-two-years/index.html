Celebrating 2 years of building HandsOnMoney Yeah, I can't believe it. First notes I took on building my financial app is dated early July 2024. Of course I overestimated myself by a mile, and of course I underestimated power of community by a hundred miles. 2 years back I was still fresh on my "getting out of debt" journey, I just started using GnuCash and was struggling to understand the concepts. I should say switching from EveryDollar to GnuCash was a pretty dramatic change! On one side a simple app that we shared with my wife. On another side is GnuCash - a next level of spreadsheet nerdery. My wife immediately stopped following the budget since it became virtually impossible for her. So I decided to make a dent in a universe - and build an app that has double-entry accounting under the hood and that is as simple as Mint.com or Money Copilot. Should I say I failed at UI? I am a backend engineer by my nature and my UI skills was somewhere here: ==meme of the weather app== I absolutely did not see how much time will go into making UI simple and approachable! I built an app and my wife is like "why do I need to create a budget every month?" So I decided to pivot. Or at least to take longer path - build an app for nerds, and then make it simple for busy moms like my wife. That's how I spent last 2 years nights: - jumping hoops of Swift - Apple's programming language. Boy is it different from Java and C#! - learning Apple's visual design - iterating on UI/UX with my wife till the moment she is refusing to answer my "can I see how you do X?" questions And of course community. I understood that I'm building a relationship rather than software. So many people pinged me over the Discord or iMessage! Everyone brought something important - idea, validation, or even a hand to help. The "people-dimension" is something I absolutely did not foresee. Being a true geek I tried to stay away from "people-stuff" at my job. However users of HandsOnMoney showed me something important - "people stuff" is more important than "software-stuff". Your software can have all the features of the competitor yet without people using it - it's just a hollow barrel. And as you may see, I'm trying. I'm forcing myself to talk about things I build, decisions I took, reflect on hard things. So where is HandsOnMoney now? It's still a super niche app. Non-nerds are still having the WTF?! impression the very moment they open the app. What is GnuCash? double-entry? expense is an account? While at the same time the geeks keep building solutions around GnuCash and HandsOnMoney to share expenses with their families. What's next? Simplicity. Community. And I hope a faster releases with more features. My wife is still asking question on how to log income, struggling with the constant need of synchronizing the data with "nerd's file" - a master copy of GnuCash file in iCloud. So I will keep simplifying screens to make them approachable even for a busy mom who does not care about double entry, while at the same time giving a tools for us nerds to keep track of finances in a true double-entry way. Also I recognize that HandsOnMoney is no longer about me or my family. It's turning into a small village phase. So I'm planning to bring more content from other people, their technical setup, finance, budgeting, etc. Also for the last 6 months i did not ship anything major or at least visibly major. My main focus was to address most burning feedback and make HandsOnMoney code testable using unit tests and UI tests. I hope I did not break your setup while doing it. My hope is that I will be able to ship more often without breaking things. Features on my priority list: 1. More reports (net worth) 2. Notes fo splits 3. Family sharing 4. "Transactions screen" and quick transaction creation using simple terms like Expense, Income, Transfer, etc. 5. Apple Wallet integration And of course I'll force myself to talk and write more. Stay tuned!
