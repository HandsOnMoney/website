I'm slow and AI doesn't help much

It's 2026. Software engineering is solved. Yet I'm working "slow". Why didn't I hop on the AI rocket and ship 100x faster?

I think we can apply this argument to this short blog post too. I'm rewriting it for the 3rd time because I don't like what comes out. GPT or Claude could do it in no time! No one gonna read it, and it will not change my thought process. BTW, I have no illusions about my "writing" - maybe no one will read it. Yet the last part is precious - I reflected while writing.

Here is how I work on HandsOnMoney. Wake up early while everyone is asleep. Crack my laptop, open Xcode, and jump into code. I am on the clock to wrap a bug fix or deliver something toward the next feature. Feedback is fast - I change one thing, and Xcode shows me why it's dumb (compilation error, button is too small) in seconds. When things are ready, hit run, and I'm testing how the change "feels" on a real device - almost instantaneously. Gosh, it feels awful, I feel dumb, and I'm going back to square one.

Almost any knowledge worker recognizes that - it's a "flow state". A state of the mind when you become single with what you are trying to build. My method to achieve described well by Cal Newport's Deep Work. I adopted his method a few years back, and it serves me extremely well.

What I described does not feel slow. Yet overall I don't ship a new feature every week. Why so?

It's not because I can't cram the code faster. I bet I can. And AI can do it in no time! The thing I build is not an app. It's my reasoning about personal finance, user experience, and what's not in the app.

So here is my theory - the value of HandsOnMoney is not in how fast I ship it, but rather in how well my thought process is aligned with yours (my user). AI can't go out, talk with people, make a conclusion, and build something viable. It's a human's job to talk with other humans, understand their problem, assess if a product can solve it, and if it general enough problem to solve. The coding part is rather small here, and manual coding prevents me from doing dumb things - because, as a human, I'm lazy.

When I close my laptop, a "slow" part kicks in (see Slow Productivity). I do my job. Raise a family, walk a dog, live a life. And somewhere in the middle comes an insight. An "aha!" moment, when a solution to a problem comes seemingly out of nowhere. Again, I have no illusions about myself. I'm not Sir Isaac Newton. My "aha" moments are small, yet they deliver value to me. It can be realizing how to structure a chart (e.g., what's best for a net worth chart - pie or bar chart?). Or it can be a realization that I can't apply the "last write wins" method used by Actual Budget because it will break the books.

So unfortunately (or fortunately), AI can't process reflection faster. It can do the absolute opposite - making me watch memes while AI is building (yes, I'm looking at you at you Chad IDE)

I absolutely agree that AI can do it in no time. And you know what? McDonald's can cook you a hamburger in no time. The thing is - the app built without a human reflecting feels exactly like a hamburger from McDonald's - a bit of a cardboard taste but has calories. I'll be honest with you - I eat McDonald's, and I use AI. But it's not my dream to be able to cook as fast as McDonalds. My dream is to solve personal finance for people like my wife and me. Turbo-nerds and just people living their lives, not knowing how beautiful an Excel spreadsheet is, might be.

P.S. I'm not Ed Zitron to say there is no value in AI. There is - dull and boring stuff like unit tests or writing "what's changed" is on AI. There is just not enough value for the core activity.
