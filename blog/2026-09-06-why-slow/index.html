Why slow?

# Who's slow? Me? If you asking me if I'm slow, I'll swear I'm not. I work on HandsOnMoney every day. Two to fours day. On weekends and holidays. I work early mornings or late nights when everyone are asleep. I remove all the possible distractions and put myself into a flow mode. Where I become single with code and a problem I'm trying to solve. I should admit though that I might be confusing business with productivity. I don't ship a major feature every week. Heck I'm happy if can ship something major at least once a month. # What's hard? I spend around 50% of time coding. Yet I do not think it's coding that's a bottleneck in shipping features faster. Application's code is like a legal contract. A wall of text made of precise language. Worked out through years and years of experience. I program for 20 years and do professional software engineering for the last 15 years. So writing code is not a big deal. I think in code so the process of typing is rather fast. It's rare when I'm getting stack seriously for more than an hour with code. Also Internet, and modern languages made this process even lot easier. Then why it takes so much time and what's that that takes so much time? It's knowledge acquisition. With HandsOnMoney I'm trying to understand how to merge two words - nerds's world of double-entry accounting and busy person world of simple finance trackers. Sure there is definite answers to things that are set in stone - like accounting equation. But there is no definite answer to things like displaying net worth chart, guiding users throught onboarding, or preventing them to make iCloud file conflicts. I'll be honest with you that was and is the most hardest challenge. And that's where majority of time goes in. I make something that I think makes sense, and then it simply looks ugly, does not make sense, or feels wrong. And it takes more iterations to make it at least more right than wrong. And the is one more time sink. Changing previous asumptions. In software engineering it's called refactoring. And surely it can be done with AI. Yet I do it manually. Because these are my assumptions that needs corrections, thus I want to be a driver seat to make sure that the new assumption do not break old and actually make sense. # Outsource? Open source? AI? So theoretically I can delegate those tasks to churn features faste: * I can hire a small team * Make HandsOnMoney open source and build a community * Or engineer a system where AI ships Each approach has it's own pros and cons. Hiring a team would test my leadership and management abilities and burn some of my personal savings. Making it open source will build more trust and hopefully bring other humans to the table, but will risk a "bazaar of ideas"-style product and dever limited attention to community building. Building with brings more questions to answers (how can I delegate dicision making to a machine that does not have my product taste, faster but prduces too much code). All those practices would take decision make from me. And that's a core problem for a prduct at it's infancy like HandsOnMoney. # Will it be like this forever? Of course no. I'm not dogmatic about coding with AI, love working with people and open to different ways of working. When I'm done with HandsOnMoney core mechanics. I'll need a lot of help shipping HandsONmoney to other platforms Android, web and desktop. And surely I'll ask for help. For now I'll stick to what I do now, slowly aquiring knowledge of what's working and what's not.
